\paragraph{Splits.}
The official test set will not appear until 10 January 2027, after the course ends, so the development set is our test set and we treat it that way: nothing is selected on it. Model selection uses slices of the training data, split by source pair so that both directions of a pair land on the same side. Train and dev share no source pair. They do share text, though. 7 unordered phrase pairs and 155 of the 712 distinct development phrases (22\%) also occur in training. We report this rather than hide it, and we come back to it when we read the scores.

\paragraph{Quality.}
The files are complete, with no missing or empty text. The one genuine defect we found is small. ``South Africans'' paired with ``South Africa'' appears twice in training, once as \FW{} and once as \NO{}, and both cannot be right. Beyond that, one-word phrases are common (563 occurrences in train), and some phrases are visibly fragments of the chunking that produced them, ``'s war shrine visit'' for instance. Whether a pair came from an image caption or a news headline is not recorded in the train and dev files. Only the 461-pair trial sample carries that field (244 caption and 217 headline pairs), so we cannot stratify by source.
