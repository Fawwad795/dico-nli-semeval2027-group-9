\begin{abstract}
SemEval-2027 Task 2 (DiCo-NLI) asks for one of four relations between two short phrases in a fixed order, and it checks whether a system's answer on a pair agrees with its answer when the pair is turned around. In Assignment 1 a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-base reached weighted F1 0.807 on the English development set and still contradicted itself on 32 to 37 of the 277 reversible pairs. Here we propose and specify a method aimed at that gap: a frozen 1.5B-parameter decoder, scored on four option letters, with low-rank adapters trained under a loss that ties the prediction for a pair to the prediction for its reverse, and a decoding step that averages the two orders. The averaging makes the system self-consistent by construction, so we judge it on weighted F1 and \HardCons{}, never on \SoftCons{} alone. The report fixes the comparison systems, the ablations, the evaluation measures and the full protocol before any full run. The method is implemented and tested end to end, and a preliminary run verifies the pipeline; its numbers are checks, not performance claims.
\end{abstract}
