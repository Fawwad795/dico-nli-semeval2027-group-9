\begin{abstract}
SemEval-2027 Task 2 (DiCo-NLI) asks for one of four relations between two short phrases in a fixed order, and it checks whether a system's answer on a pair agrees with its answer when the pair is turned around. In Assignment 1 a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-base reached weighted F1 0.807 on the English development set and still contradicted itself on 32 to 37 of the 277 reversible pairs. Here we propose and specify a method aimed at that gap: a frozen 1.5B-parameter decoder, scored on four option letters, with low-rank adapters trained under a loss that ties the prediction for a pair to the prediction for its reverse, and a decoding step that averages the two orders. The averaging raises self-consistency by design, so we judge it on weighted F1 and \HardCons{}, never on \SoftCons{} alone. The report fixes the comparison systems, the ablations, the evaluation measures and the full protocol before any full run. The method is implemented and tested end to end. A one-seed preliminary run verifies the pipeline: the adapters train in about seven minutes on one GPU, and the untrained model either answers \EQ{} to almost everything or contradicts itself on 237 of 277 pairs, depending on its size. The run also disproved one of our own claims, that the averaging step makes \SoftCons{} exactly 1. Its numbers check the pipeline, and we make no performance claim from them.
\end{abstract}
